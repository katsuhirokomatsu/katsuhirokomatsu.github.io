Partially Coasean Temporary Layoffs

Katsuhiro Komatsu
Kyoto University
November 15, 2026

Download PDF

Abstract

Whether employment decisions reflect the joint interests of firms and workers is fundamental to understanding labor market responses to shocks and policies. This paper provides a way to measure the prevalence of joint decisions, using temporary layoffs under U.S. unemployment insurance, where benefits raise workers’ gains from layoffs but impose experience-rated costs on firms. Higher benefits therefore reduce layoffs under unilateral decisions but increase them under joint decisions. Changes in state benefit schedules reveal that benefits increase temporary layoffs. Combining this response with existing estimates of the impact of experience rating, I find that joint decisions are common at the margin: temporary layoffs are partially Coasean, and models in which firms decide alone overstate how rigid wages transmit shocks into them.

JEL Classification: J63, J64, J65

Keywords: Temporary Layoffs; Unemployment Insurance; Experience Rating; Efficient Separations; Wage Rigidity
