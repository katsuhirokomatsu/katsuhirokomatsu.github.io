Partially Coasean Temporary Layoffs

Katsuhiro Komatsu
Kyoto University
October 27, 2026

Download PDF

Abstract

Whether employment decisions reflect the joint interests of firms and workers is fundamental to understanding labor market responses to shocks and policies. I study temporary layoffs under U.S. unemployment insurance, where benefits raise workers’ gains from layoffs but impose experience-rated costs on firms. These opposing incentives favor fewer layoffs under unilateral decisions but more under joint decisions. Changes in state schedules reveal that benefits increase temporary layoffs, whereas experience rating reduces them. A model combining these responses implies that 12% of marginal decisions are joint: temporary layoffs are only partially Coasean, yet this minority reverses their response to benefits.

Keywords: Temporary Layoffs; Unemployment Insurance; Experience Rating; Efficient Separations; Wage Rigidity
